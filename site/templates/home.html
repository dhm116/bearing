One question, two ways

Acme's payments service is slow, and you need whoever looks after it. Here is how that tends to go today, and how it goes with Bearing.

Today

  1. 0 min Code repository

    A file says changes need approval from @acme/payments. That's a team. Which person?

    Partial
  2. 6 min Team wiki

    The service page says Sam owns it. Last edited two years ago.

    Out of date
  3. 11 min Company directory

    Dead end
  4. 15 min Team chat

    “Does anyone know who looks after payments?” No reply yet.

    Waiting
  5. 34 min A colleague

Questions people ask every day

Bearing is for anyone who works on or around software: developers, the person on call, new joiners, team leads, and the AI assistants that now write code alongside them. Each answer below comes from the demo.

  • Who owns this?

    payments-api: the payments team.

    GitHub · CODEOWNERS

  • What does this depend on, and how?

    billing-api: the postgres database on port 5432, and the ledger service over HTTP.

    Kubernetes

  • Which services use an old version of Go?

    Below 1.22: payments-api, on 1.21. One repository's version couldn't be read, so it's listed apart rather than guessed.

What makes it different

  • Works with what you have

    Bearing reads the tools you already use, and sits beside catalogs like Backstage instead of replacing them. Nobody has to fill in a new catalog or remember to keep it up to date.

  • Open, and yours to shape

    Free and open source, and every part can be swapped for one that suits your organization.
